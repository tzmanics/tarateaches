## The situation

Templates are a company's handshake: the first code a developer runs, the first impression of whether the platform respects their time. At Netlify, the official templates had to track a fast-moving product *and* a fast-moving framework ecosystem — every upstream change could quietly break a first impression.

I built and led the team responsible for that handshake, during a period when both the product and the company kept changing shape underneath us.

## What I actually did

- Hired and grew a team with a clear identity: we are the developer's first five minutes, and we take that personally.
- Replaced heroics with process. Template quality went from "someone notices and scrambles" to scheduled checks, clear ownership, and a definition of done that included docs and deploy-button behavior — not just code that compiles.
- Kept communication boring on purpose. Predictable standups, honest status, no surprises rolling uphill or downhill.

## What it took

Leading through re-orgs, shifting priorities, and layoff seasons — the stretch where a manager's real job is absorbing ambiguity and handing the team back something they can act on. Not pretending things were stable, but translating instability into "here's what we control, here's what we ship this week."

## What came out of it

A team that stayed intact, kept shipping, and trusted each other enough to disagree in the open. The templates became a system with a maintenance rhythm instead of a pile with an owner. And I came out with my core leadership belief field-tested: teams don't need less change — they need change metabolized into a plan.
