Learners pay for education in the one currency they can't earn more of: time. Before I build anything — a course, a workshop, a docs page, a two-minute video — I run it against a short list of debts. If the content can't pay them, it doesn't ship.

## Debt 1: An outcome the learner can say out loud

Not "understand serverless functions." That's a vibe. The version that counts is a sentence the learner can say afterward and mean: "I can deploy a function, pass it data, and debug it when it 500s." If I can't write that sentence before building, I'm not ready to build.

## Debt 2: The shortest honest path

Respecting time means ruthless cutting — but honest cutting. There's a version of "quick and easy" that quietly skips the hard parts and abandons the learner at the first real error. The shortest *honest* path includes the failure they'll actually hit, and what to do about it. I'd rather teach one workflow with its top three failure modes than three workflows that all assume sunshine.

## Debt 3: Something that survives contact with production

Education that only works in the sandbox is a demo, not a curriculum. Every piece of content I build has to end with the learner holding something real: a deployed app, a working config, a decision they can defend in a design review. Good education is engineering — you find where understanding breaks and fix the failure point, then you put it in production with real people.

## Debt 4: Dignity

No trick questions. No "obviously." No prerequisite-shaming. The fastest way to lose a learner is to make them feel stupid for being a learner — and the communities I built taught me that the people who almost quit are usually one kind explanation away from becoming your best mentors. Dignity isn't the soft part of curriculum design. It's a retention strategy with a conscience.

## The test I trust most

Six weeks later, is the learner still using the thing? Not "did they finish," not "did they rate it five stars" — did it change what they can do on a Tuesday afternoon when nobody's watching? That's the debt fully paid. Everything else is engagement metrics wearing a graduation cap.
